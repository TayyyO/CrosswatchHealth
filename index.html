See medical device safety and supply problems together, and earlier.

CrossWatch links FDA adverse event, recall, device identifier and shortage data for each device category, so hospitals, distributors and manufacturers can spot the categories where safety and supply trouble are building at the same time.

The problem

FDA publishes the warning signs in separate places, so they are rarely read together.

Device safety signals (adverse event reports, recalls) and supply signals (shortages, discontinuances, how many manufacturers make a device) live in different FDA systems. Teams that make purchasing, substitution and quality decisions see problems late and in pieces.

Shortages

The FDA shortage list is checked by hand and occasionally, or a shortage is first noticed as a supplier backorder.

Recalls

Recall notices arrive one at a time and are matched to inventory by hand.

Adverse events

MAUDE reports for the categories a team buys are rarely reviewed, because the database is hard to query and interpret.

The overlap

There is no simple way to see whether a category already under supply pressure is also showing rising safety activity.

How it works

Link the sources, score each category, and send the result to a person for review.

Step 1

Link public FDA data by product code

Every source is joined on FDA's three-letter product code into one table per device category per month.

MAUDE adverse eventsRecallsEnforcement reportsClassificationGUDID device identifiersShortages ListDiscontinuance List
Step 2

Score five explainable signals

Each category is compared with its own history, using only data available at the time. The signals combine into a 0 to 100 score with every component visible.

Step 3

Flag convergence for expert review

The main flag is raised when safety and supply signals are elevated in the same month. Automated text analysis groups recall reasons into themes and drafts a review note, so the reviewer starts from a summary rather than raw records.

S1

Adverse event trend

The last three months of reports compared with the prior two years for the same category.

S2

Severity mix

A rising share of deaths and injuries among all reports.

S3

Recall activity

Recalls in the last 12 months, weighted by FDA recall class.

S4

List status

On FDA's shortage or discontinuance list, or recently removed.

S5

Supply concentration

How few labelers have devices in commercial distribution.

Early findings

We test the method against shortages that already ended, and report what we find.

Each completed shortage is rescored month by month with only the data available at the time, leaving out the shortage listing itself. Results are reported as found, including when no signal came first.

FJK · listed Mar 2025 · removed Jun 2026

Hemodialysis bloodlines

No safety signal came before the listing. Adverse event reports crossed their threshold five months after it, and a Class I recall followed. Here the safety data confirmed the problem during the shortage rather than predicting it.

MDB · listed Jul 2024

Blood culture bottles

A brief rise in reports nine months before listing faded by January 2024. Because this shortage came from a supplier's plastic bottle problem, the early rise was probably unrelated. Shortages like this are why CrossWatch scores supply signals alongside safety.

Next: a control comparison against categories that never went into shortage, which measures how often flags appear without a shortage following.

Principles

Open enough that another analyst can check every number.

Public data only

No confidential, proprietary or patient-level data. Every input is published by FDA.

Transparent scoring

Every score breaks down into its components. Methods and weights are documented publicly.

Reproducible

Code, configuration and source versions are versioned so the pipeline can be rerun from the README.

A prompt, not a verdict

CrossWatch does not diagnose defects, forecast shortages or give purchasing advice. It supports FDA, manufacturer and hospital surveillance.

About

An independent research and development project led by Qasim T. Arigbabu.

CrossWatch develops AI-enabled analytics and decision-support tools that link medical device safety data with supply and shortage data, so the critical devices U.S. patients depend on stay available and safely distributed. It is not yet a company and does not sell products or services.

Review the method

Practitioners in device quality, regulatory affairs and hospital supply chain are invited to review the signals, the tiers and a sample of flagged categories. Written feedback shapes the next version of the method.

The interactive prototype shows the current ranking, each category's signals, and the full retrospective test.
