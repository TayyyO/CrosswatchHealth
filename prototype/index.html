Read with care. Report counts are not rates. MAUDE is a passive reporting system. The score is a prompt for expert review, not a finding that a device is unsafe, a shortage forecast, or purchasing advice.

Which device categories need attention, and why

Every category on the FDA Medical Device Shortages List in the scoring month, plus recently removed and contrast categories. Each score adds a safety subscore (adverse event trend, severity mix, recalls) and a supply subscore (list status, supplier concentration). Select a category for its detail.

Did any signal come before the official listing?

Each completed shortage is rescored month by month using only data received by that month, with list status (S4) left out. The results below are reported as found, including the case where no signal came first.

What these two cases show so far

Hemodialysis bloodlines (FJK), quality-related. No safety signal crossed its threshold before the March 2025 listing. The adverse event trend crossed in August 2025, five months after listing, and a Class I recall followed in September 2025. In this case the safety data confirmed the problem during the shortage rather than warning ahead of it.

Blood culture bottles (MDB), supply-driven. The adverse event trend crossed in October 2023, nine months before the July 2024 listing, then fell back below threshold by January 2024. The shortage was linked to a supplier's plastic bottle availability, so this early crossing is probably unrelated to the cause. It is counted as a flag, but it should not be read as a successful early warning.

Method finding for v0.2. With S4 excluded, the supply subscore can reach at most 0.4, so the convergence flag cannot be raised in a prediction test. Either the convergence rule needs a no-S4 variant, or S5 needs a time-varying history from archived GUDID releases. This is recorded for the changelog.

Still to run: the control comparison against never-listed product codes, the severity signal (S2) in the monthly back-test, and the remaining completed shortages in the list history.

How the score is built

Risk Scoring Specification v0.1. All weights and thresholds are starting values, changed only through the documented validation process.

Signals and thresholds

SignalMeasureElevated when
S1 Adverse event trendMean of last 3 months vs mean and SD of the prior 24z at least 2.0 and current level at least 5 per month
S2 Severity mixDeath and injury share, last 6 months vs prior 24Rise of at least 10 points with at least 5 death or injury reports
S3 Recall activityWeighted recall events in 12 months (Class I 3, II 2, III 1, unknown 2)Any Class I, or weighted count at least 4
S4 List statusShortage list 1.0, discontinuance 0.6, removed in last 12 months 0.4Confirmed supply problem
S5 Supply concentrationLabelers in commercial distribution: 1 = 1.0, 2 = 0.7, 3 to 4 = 0.4, 5+ = 0.1Context signal

Combining

Safety = 0.5 x S1 + 0.2 x S2 + 0.3 x S3 Supply = 0.6 x S4 + 0.4 x S5 Score = 100 x (0.5 x Safety + 0.5 x Supply) High score 60+ or convergence Elevated 35 to 59 Watch 15 to 34 Low below 15

Convergence is raised when Safety and Supply are both at least 0.5 in the same month.

Implementation choices to confirm

  • S1 and S2 components: 0 below threshold, then value divided by twice the threshold, capped at 1. An elevated signal therefore starts at 0.5. The specification's wording could also mean starting at 0.
  • S1 uses the Poisson test when the baseline mean is below 10 reports per month.
  • Recall class comes from enforcement reports joined on event number. Events without a resolved class use the "unknown" weight of 2.
  • S5 uses current labeler counts only. The 12-month drop adjustment needs archived GUDID releases and is not applied yet.
